## Strided held-out evaluation (`cfg.eval_stride`), branch `feat/2026-07-26-eval-stride`

`vfe3/data/datasets.py`, `vfe3/config.py`, `train_vfe3.py`, `ablation.py`, `scaling.py`,
`vfe3/viz/report.py`, `tests/test_eval_stride.py` (+30 tests), plus three test stubs widened for the
new keyword.

**Why.** With disjoint held-out windows (`stride == seq_len`, the only thing the eval path could do)
the first target of every window is predicted from one token of context, the second from two, and so
on. The reported cross-entropy is inflated by that starvation, and the inflation grows as `seq_len`
shrinks -- at `max_seq_len = 128` the starved head is a large fraction of each window, not the small
one it would be at 1024.

**What.** `eval_stride` selects the standard sliding-window evaluation for validation and test: each
window still runs on its full `seq_len` of context, but only its final `stride` targets are scored
and the overlapping prefix is masked to `-100`. Every transition is therefore counted exactly ONCE,
at `seq_len - stride` tokens of context or better. The masked prefix is context, not padding --
`train.evaluate` already runs the model on the whole window and weights it by the count of
non-ignored targets, and `targets` never touch the E-step (only the decode and CE), so masking
changes what is averaged without perturbing inference. This is the recipe in HuggingFace's
fixed-length-model perplexity documentation.

**The exactly-once contract survives.** The recorded plan was to disable `pad_final` on the strided
path, which would have dropped the tail and given up exactly-once coverage. That turned out to be
unnecessary: `pad_final` already means "cover every transition once, padding and masking as needed",
and it generalizes to overlapping windows directly. Window `i` scores transitions
`[i*stride + seq_len - stride, i*stride + seq_len)`, which tile the stream contiguously from zero;
`n` is the fewest windows whose last one reaches the final transition, and the existing tail padding
handles the overrun. At `stride == seq_len` every formula reduces exactly to the old one
(`n == ceil(transitions / seq_len)`, mask covers zero positions), so `eval_stride=None` is
byte-identical to the previous behavior. Verified exactly-once at eleven shapes including the ragged
and single-window degenerate cases, and end-to-end through a real `DataLoader`: 400 tokens give 399
scored targets at stride 8, 4, 2 and 1 alike. `stride > seq_len` still fails closed under the eval
contract (it would leave gaps no mask can recover); the train contract, which shuffles and drops its
tail, still allows it.

**One live hazard fixed in passing.** `.to(torch.long)` is a no-op for an int64 stream, so the
returned `targets` is a VIEW into the corpus tensor. Masking it in place would have silently
corrupted the stream for every later window. The masking path clones first, and a test pins that
iterating leaves the stream bit-identical.

**Wiring.** All three drivers that publish held-out numbers honor it -- `train_vfe3._select_loader`,
`ablation.get_loader`, `scaling.get_loader` -- for validation/test only; train is never strided. The
stride is in each memoizing loader's cache key, without which a sweep over `eval_stride` would be
served the first arm's loader and every arm would report identical windows. `ablation`'s
extrapolation probe uses the same stride as the cell's own metric, so the CE-vs-N curve and the
headline PPL stay one measurement at different N. `vfe3/viz/report._build_loader` is deliberately NOT
strided and now says so: it feeds diagnostic figures, not a metric, and overlapping windows would
make consecutive figure batches near-duplicates. Provenance needed no change -- `run_artifacts`
already validates `stride`/`pad_final` generically, so the eval contract lands in the run record and
a resume under a different one is correctly flagged as drift.

**Comparability, which matters more than the code.** A strided number is a DIFFERENT METRIC from a
disjoint one and is always lower. It must not be compared against the existing baselines (55.41, the
172-vs-139 pair), which were all measured disjoint. The default stays `None` precisely so the whole
recorded history remains the comparable series; set `eval_stride` when you want a properly-contexted
figure to put beside an external baseline, and say which contract produced any number you report.
Measured on a period-5 stream a small model learns: CE 0.00114 disjoint against 0.00056 at
`stride = seq_len/2`, the starved window heads and nothing else.

## A-02, the half the challenge tier left open

`vfe3/geometry/transport.py`, `vfe3/run_artifacts.py`, `tests/test_audit_fixes_2026_07_26.py`
(+6 tests). The duel re-scoped A-02 to `rope_full_gauge=False`, where "the mean transports under
`R_i Omega_ij R_j^T` while the covariance transports under bare `Omega_ij` -- no single frame works.
Needs its own check; neither side established it." Established.

**Measured** (K=4, N=4, two 2x2 blocks, flat cocycle plus a block-diagonal orthogonal rotation). The
transported mean equals the fully-rotated one to **0.0** and differs from the un-rotated one by 5.63;
the transported covariance equals the **un-rotated** one to **0.0** and differs from the rotated one
by 2.06. So the geometer's claim is exactly right: the transported pair is the pushforward of the key
under NO single linear operator, and the literal coupling-energy contract
`E_ij = D(q_i || Omega_ij# q_j)` has no `Omega_ij` that satisfies it under means-only RoPE.

**It is also not a truncation of the right object.** Against `diag(R_i Omega Sigma Omega^T R_i^T)` --
the diagonal projection of the genuinely rotated pushforward, which is what the diagonal family's
documented congruence truncation would produce -- the shipped covariance is off by a relative
**6.33**. So it cannot be filed under the existing controlled-approximation story; it is a different
object.

**What survives, and why this is not a numerical bug.** At `i = j` both `Omega_ii` and `R_i R_i^T`
are the identity, so `mu_t[i,i] == mu_i` and `sigma_t[i,i] == sigma_i` EXACTLY (measured 0.0 on both
slots). The structural `E_ii = 0` self-pair, and the `pair_mask` that keys off it, are untouched. The
asymmetry is forced rather than accidental: for a diagonal family `R Sigma R^T` is dense and
unrepresentable, which is precisely why `rope_full_gauge=True` is validated to require
`family='gaussian_full'`. Means-only is therefore a deliberate modeling choice, and changing its
semantics is the user's call, not a cleanup.

**What was actually missing was the record.** A run using means-only RoPE published nothing saying
its pair energy was a hybrid, while the analogous approximation on the covariant transport has
carried `regime_ii_covariant_exactness` all along. Added `rope_pair_energy_exactness` to
`config_toggles` -- `not_applicable` / `means_only_unrotated_covariance` / `exact_rope_congruence` --
and stated the asymmetry, with its measurements, at the `transport_covariance` seam. The purity
ledger needed no change: `gauge_flags.no_positional_rotation` already puts any RoPE run off the
gauge-pure path.

## The attention the E-step actually used, as its own figure set (R-3)

The rope investigation found the shipped `attention/` heatmaps score beta at each block's OUTPUT
belief -- after the E-step has moved it and after `head_mixer`/`block_norm`. That is a post-hoc
re-score of a belief the E-step already displaced, not the pattern that produced the aggregation;
measured max row difference on a trained checkpoint is 1.0, i.e. rows where the picture puts all mass
on a key the model gave none.

Rather than change what the existing figures mean, a second set is written beside them. `attention/`
is byte-identical and remains what `diagnostics` reports.

`VFEModel.attention_maps` gains `score_at`, default `"converged"` (the shipped path, pinned
byte-identical by test). `"entry"` scores at the belief ENTERING the block -- at `n_e_steps=1` that IS
the model's attention. `"prior"` zeroes the coupling energy, leaving the attention prior alone as the
reference for how much of a map is content. The scoring block was lifted into one local closure so
the two points can differ only by WHERE it is called. A diagnostic snapshot stores only the converged
maps, so the other two arms raise rather than silently returning the wrong thing.

`attention_estep/` receives, per eval step, one heatmap per (layer, head) at the entry belief plus a
`step_<N>_profile.png`: row-mean beta against key distance `|i-j|`, with all three arms overlaid.
That panel is the one that makes positional pathology legible -- a rotary kernel that revives at long
lag is an off-diagonal bump here and is invisible in a log-scaled heatmap, and a beta collapsed onto
its prior is three curves lying on top of each other. Each lag is normalized by the number of query
rows that HAVE it, so the tail is not damped by shrinking support, and the y-range is bounded by the
well-sampled half so a one-sample tail plunging twenty decades cannot compress the near field.

**Recorded caveat, on the figure itself.** The profile is a POSITIONAL summary, not a content test:
averaging over query rows at fixed lag hides content that is not distance-aligned. On the 2026-07-26
rope pair the three curves nearly coincide while pointwise `max|entry - prior|` is still 0.36.
Coincident curves prove only that content moves no lag-aligned mass.

the heatmaps did not); and the emission is wrapped so a replay failure is logged and swallowed like
every other figure, since the snapshot cannot supply the new arms.

## `rope_insertion`: the rotation now folds into the gauge frame (R-4)

The rope investigation's R-4 said the positional rotation is inserted on the wrong side of the
learned frame. Before building the fix I checked it, and **the correction as both agents worded it
is wrong**. Gauge covariance `||Omega^g - g Omega g^-1||`:

| operator | residual |
|---|---|
| `R_i Omega R_j^T` (shipped, left) | 5.59 |
| `Omega R_i R_j^T` (as the agents worded it) | 4.21 |
| `U_i R_i^T R_j U_j^-1` (frame form) | **1.4e-06** |

Only the frame form works, and it is not a wrapper change: `V_i = U_i R_i^T` makes rope an ordinary
flat coboundary, so every downstream contraction, fast path and cocycle certification applies to it
unchanged. `cfg.rope_insertion` selects `"right"` (default, this form; the relative rotation sits
between the query and key content factors per `GL(K)_attention.tex` and Su et al. Eq 16) or `"left"`
(the shipped composition, retained so pre-2026-07-26 rope runs reproduce, with a warning quantifying
what it costs). The toggle is exposed in `train_vfe3.py`.

Measured against a NON-IDENTITY frame, which is the case the shipped `tests/test_rope.py` could never
see because every one of its rope tests uses `Omega = I`, exactly where the two insertions coincide:
right is exactly relative (residual < 1e-5 against left's > 1e-2) and exactly gauge covariant
(< 1e-4 against left's > 1e-1).

**Where `right` is unavailable, the config REJECTS rather than falling back.** The fold needs a
per-vertex frame. Measured which container each mode actually builds rather than assuming: `flat`
gives `FactoredTransport`, `regime_ii`/`regime_ii_covariant` give a dense tensor,
`regime_ii_link` gives a bare `DirectLinkTransport` and `regime_ii_link_charted` a charted one (which
folds -- the edge factor just sits between the frames). `TransportRegistration.rope_right_foldable`
declares it, defaulting False so a new transport opts in. One further condition is not a property of
the transport at all: `flat` builds the FACTORED container only for a group with MORE THAN ONE irrep
block, so `glk`/`so_k` fall to dense even on `flat` and are rejected too.

**`rope_on_value=True` is the safer pairing on the gradient path, and it is NOT the better one
overall.** It keeps the config on the closed-form kernel route, so `mm_exact` stays legal and the R-1
freeze cannot occur at all -- there is no detached oracle to sever the gauge table's only gradient
path. `rope_on_value=False` still needs `oracle_unroll_grad=True`.

> [!warning] Corrected 2026-07-26 by measurement. This paragraph originally read "`rope_on_value=True`

## `check_parameter_motion`: did every trainable table actually move?

**The premise I started from was wrong, and the correction is the more useful result.** I proposed
this as "nothing in the codebase detects a silently frozen parameter, which is how R-1 cost 175 PPL."
Measured on a tiny model under R-1's own config (`rope` + `rope_on_value=False` +
`oracle_unroll_grad=False`), that is false three times over: `parameter_report` returns
`phi_embed.grad is None` and puts it in `dead_names`; `_banner` prints
`dead under config (no grad): phi_embed, ...`; and config validation emits a warning naming
`prior_bank.phi_embed` and prescribing `oracle_unroll_grad=True`. `build_optimizer` separately
asserts exact parameter-group coverage. R-1 was detected and printed. It was missed in the reading.

What none of them covers is the OUTCOME. All three are capability checks at init: they establish that
a gradient CAN flow, never that the parameter MOVED. The gap is reachable and was measured, not
argued -- with `m_phi_lr=0.0`, `parameter_report` reports `phi_embed` live while it moves exactly
`0.0` across real optimizer steps. A zero group LR, a vanishing-but-nonzero gradient, and a parameter
live at step 0 that dies later all produce R-1's outcome through a mechanism no existing guard sees.

`snapshot_parameters` clones the parameters to the HOST before the first step (no device memory), and
`parameter_motion_report` compares at the end, on the RAW iterate before `ema.copy_to`, so the answer
is about the optimizer's updates rather than the average of them. Motion is `||p - p_0||` relative to
`||p_0||`; for a zero-init table (`connection_W`, `mixer_deltas`, `path_weights`) the relative form is
undefined, so absolute motion is the criterion and any nonzero value counts as trained. Only
`requires_grad` parameters can be flagged, so the deliberately fixed centroid `r` under
`learnable_r=False` is never reported.

**The split that keeps it readable.** A first pass warned on `decode_log_scale` under the repo default
`use_prior_bank=False`, where that table is config-dead by construction and the init probe already
says so. A guard that fires on every run of an intentional ablation is a guard that gets tuned out --
which is the actual mechanism by which R-1 went unread for 15000 steps. So the immobile set is split:
parameters already known dead go to `frozen_known_dead` (recorded, never warned), leaving `frozen`
for the ones that received a gradient and still did not move. The dead set is read off the FIRST REAL
STEP -- `train_step` zeroes grads on entry and not on exit, so `.grad` is still live when it returns.
That costs no extra forward/backward and reflects the graph the run actually trains through rather
than a synthetic probe's approximation of it; it is gated on `did_step` so the nonfinite-grad path,
which drops grads, cannot read as "everything is dead" and mute the check.

`check_parameter_motion=True` by default (cost is one host-side clone per run),
`parameter_motion_rel_tol=1e-6`. A real freeze is exact -- AdamW skips a parameter whose grad is
`None`, and a zero LR scales the update to zero -- so the tolerance only has to clear fp32 round-off,
not separate "slow" from "stopped". The report is written to `parameter_motion.json` in the run
artifacts.

The guard already fires as a TRUE positive inside the existing suite: `_terminal_cfg` and the timing
test both set `m_phi_lr=0.0`, and both correctly report `pos_phi_free` and `prior_bank.phi_embed`
frozen.

**Lanes:** CPU fast **`tests=4413 failures=0 errors=0 skipped=28`**, CUDA **`tests=24 failures=0`**
(on `2.10.0.dev20251210+cu128`, `cuda.is_available()=True`), CPU slow `tests=3 failures=1` (the known
pre-existing `test_finalize_skips_figures_when_disabled`). +15 tests in
`tests/test_parameter_motion_20260726.py`. Two `_Artifacts` test doubles in
`test_audit_diagnostic_memory_20260720.py` gained the `save_json` their real counterpart already has.

## mu_weight_decay, D-08, and the categorical emission factor

**`mu_weight_decay`** (`config.py`, `Optional[float] = None`) is the exact mirror of
`sigma_weight_decay` for the mean-role capacity tables, threaded in `train.py` to `mu_embed`,
`s_mu_embed`, `decode_mu_embed`, and `output_proj_weight`. `None` inherits the global decay, so the
default is byte-identical. It closes Tier-1 item 1.4's one gap: `s_mu_embed` is the live mean table
under `prior_source='model_channel'` and previously had no `weight_decay` key and no config route at
all. Expressed in `train_vfe3.py` at `0.0`. Pinned by `tests/test_mu_weight_decay.py` (4 tests),
including the end-to-end case that an unvisited row does not shrink.

**D-08** is fixed at all three sites. `model.py` used `(phi**2).mean()` while
`e_step.phi_alignment_loss` used `(phi**2).sum()`, so one `mass_phi` named two strengths differing by
`phi.numel()`; and `free_energy_value` accepted-and-ignored the knob, so a sweep logged an F omitting
the term the phi substep descends. All three now read the per-position squared norm: the E-step and
the reported F sum it over the sequence, the M-step averages it over positions to match `ce`. Latent
at the `0.0` default. `test_phase0_forward_beliefs.py::_E_LOSS` re-pinned 2.7726650238 ->
2.7731170654; verified the new penalty is exactly 8.000x the old at that config's `n_gen=8`, so the

`tests/test_mass_phi_normalization_20260726.py` (4 tests).

**Categorical emission factor** (`emission_mode` in `off|shared|separate`, `emission_weight`, both
default OFF). VFE 4.0 `eq:state-model-markov-blanket-potentials` puts `log L(x_s|z_s,m_s)` inside the
